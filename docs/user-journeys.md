## What they share

- Every journey starts with a course code, a course name, a programme, or nothing. One search box that matches courses and programmes covers all of them.
- Every journey compares the same four numbers: pass rate, size, average grade, and trend. The list row and the course header show exactly these.
- Journeys 2, 3 and 5 need individual exam dates. The timeline on the course page covers them.
- A programme is a scope on the list, not a separate page.

## Structure

- **List**: search, programme scope, and every course in one sortable list. Names show in English or Swedish, as picked in the header, and search matches them in either. Active courses come first. Ended courses sit in a collapsed group at the end.
- **Planner**: a programme, found by searching its code or its name in English or Swedish, then a year of study and optionally a track. The year's study periods sit on top as boxes with what every student takes already in them: the compulsory courses, and the thesis when it can be done in only one way. Each box counts its credits against the 15 of full-time study. Below them, the courses to choose between in one study period at a time, sorted by rating or pass rate, with both shown as in the list. Each course shows its credits. Adding a course puts it in its study periods, and a course that runs over two is marked so, shows how many of its credits fall in the listed period, and counts in each what it gives there. A course already in the year in another study period is greyed out, and adding it there moves it. A course picked in another year is greyed out with its year. The planner says when two courses share lecture times, when only one of two courses counts towards the degree, and how far the courses of every year go towards a compulsory elective rule, also a course the plan lists outside the rule in another year. A dot marks the compulsory electives. Below those rules sit the degree requirements the student has to choose courses for, each with how far the compulsory courses and the picks of every year go towards it. A course that counts towards one says so: a label with its credits in the theme, or a diamond for a course of a list. The marks fade once the requirement is met. Courses without results link to their syllabus on chalmers.se. A civil engineering programme can add a master's programme after year 3, from a dashed button beside the years. It opens a list of the master's programmes the programme syllabus accredits, each with its tracks, its compulsory credits and courses, its number of electives, the mean pass rate and rating of its courses with results, and behind a button the courses and subjects its syllabus asks students to have studied before it. The chosen one adds years 4 and 5 with its own plan, and the degree requirements count its courses too. Its prerequisites stay behind a button under its name above every year, so the years before it can be planned to meet them. A button after year 5 changes it, and the picks of each master's programme stay remembered. The picks and the master's programme live in the URL, so a plan can be shared. The browser also remembers them for each programme and year, and the planner link in the header returns to the programme and year last planned. Picks and a master's programme in a URL win over remembered ones.
- **Course**: this year's study periods with their examiners and what the programmes that plan the course ask of it, then the four numbers, the timeline at full width, then grades, other parts, the course survey, and other instances of the course. The survey lists every round and sums up the newest one, or another round or all of them when picked in the list.

## Definitions

- Pass rate: passed attempts divided by all attempts on the main exam, over the last three academic years with results.
- Size: attempts per year on the main exam, over the same three years.
- Ended: no new results for 18 months.
- Main exam: the exam part with the most attempts. Courses without an exam use their largest part.
- Rating: the mean answer to "What is your overall impression of the course?" in Chalmers course surveys, 1 to 5, over every answer in the last five academic years. Courses with fewer than five answers have no rating. The survey reports only show how many gave each answer as a bar chart per question, so `pnpm evaluations` reads the bar lengths of every standard question and checks them against the reported mean.
- Spread: marks for the rating and the other survey questions shade each answer by its share, darkest from 60% of answers, blending between neighbouring answers, with a tick at the median. Average grade shows only its number, since the pass rate bar beside it shows how the grades spread. Each whole step of the scale gets an equal slot, so values at the ends sit inside the mark. Mean, median and standard deviation show on hover.
- Compulsory elective: an elective course in a choice of the programme plan that asks for at least some of its courses. The plans have no rule of their own for it.
- Degree requirement: what the programme syllabus asks of the whole degree on top of the yearly plans. The planner knows two kinds: credits in a theme, which is MTS (Human, technology and society) or Environment and sustainable development, and at least one course of a list. A course often gives fewer theme credits than its own credits. The planner leaves out a requirement that the programme's compulsory courses meet on their own, since it asks nothing of the student. A requirement that a master's programme's compulsory courses meet stays, shown as met, since the choice of master's programme meets it. A programme without a syllabus for the year takes its newest one from the five years before.
- Master's programme in the planner: a civil engineering programme's fourth and fifth year, whatever years its own plan has this year. The programme syllabus lists the accredited ones. A plan's picks are kept by the master's programme and its own year, so the same picks show whether it is planned alone or after a programme.
- Block: Chalmers' timetable slot of a course round, A to D. Two courses in the same block in the same study period have lectures at the same times. A plus, as in "B+", adds hours to the block, so the planner counts it as the block. About half of the rounds have a block.
- Thesis in the planner: thesis courses that run in the same study periods for the same credits fill the year alike, so they show as one. A year with one such thesis has it in the year from the start. A year with several, such as a thesis of 30 or of 60 credits, lists them to choose between.
- Workload: the course survey question on workload, where 1 is too low, 3 balanced and 5 too high. It stays out of the rating.
