# User journeys

## Journeys

| #   | User                      | Story                                                                                         | Entry                     | Ends on                                           |
| --- | ------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------- |
| 1   | Student picking electives | Compare the courses I am considering, so I avoid one where half fail or students are unhappy. | Programme or course names | List, sorted, then one or two courses             |
| 2   | Student before an exam    | See how recent exams went, so I know what to expect.                                          | Course code               | Course, latest exams                              |
| 3   | Student planning a retake | See when retakes happen and how they go.                                                      | Course code               | Course, small exams in the timeline               |
| 4   | New student               | See which courses in my programme are hard.                                                   | Programme name or code    | List scoped to the programme, sorted by pass rate |
| 5   | Student representative    | Spot courses in my programme where students grew less happy.                                  | Programme                 | List scoped to the programme, sorted by trend     |
| 6   | Curious visitor           | Find the hardest and easiest courses at Chalmers.                                             | Nothing                   | List, sorted by pass rate, large courses only     |
| 7   | Student choosing courses  | See every course I can take next year, when it runs and what my plan asks of me.              | Programme and year        | Planner, then one or two courses                  |

## What they share

- Every journey starts with a course code, a course name, a programme, or nothing. One search box that matches courses and programmes covers all of them. It matches examiners too.
- Every journey compares the same four numbers: pass rate, size, average grade, and trend. The list row and the course header show exactly these.
- Journeys 2, 3 and 5 need individual exam dates. The timeline on the course page covers them.
- A programme is a scope on the list, not a separate page. So is an examiner.

## Structure

- **List**: search, a programme or examiner scope, study periods, and every course in one sortable list. The site, course and programme names included, shows in English or Swedish, as picked in the header, and search matches names in either. Search suggests each programme it matches as a scope, and a programme in a row, on a course page or at the top of the planner leads to the same scope. Search also matches this year's examiners: it suggests each one as a scope, and a course row that matched by an examiner names them. An examiner's name on a course page leads to the same scope. The study period buttons keep the courses that run in any of the picked periods this year. Active courses come first. Courses without results come after them, by name: the courses of this year's plans that stats.ftek.se has no results for, and the new courses of upcoming master's programmes. They have no page here, so they link to chalmers.se: to their syllabus, or to the course list that names a course without a code. A thesis that only a course list names stays out, since every master's programme lists one. Ended courses sit in a collapsed group at the end. The survey column shows the rating unless another course survey question is picked from the chevron beside its header. Picking one sorts by it, and the column keeps it while the list sorts by another column.
- **Planner**: a programme, found by searching its code or its name in English or Swedish, then the year the student started it, a year of study and its track, if it has any. A track is a specialisation in a master's programme and a concentration in the programme before it. The planner asks for the start year before it shows a plan, when the programme admitted students in several years and neither the link nor the browser names one. A year with tracks asks which one the student follows, or none, which shows the courses of every track. The browser remembers the track chosen in each plan, so a master's programme keeps it in both its years. The year's study periods sit on top as boxes with what every student takes already in them: the compulsory courses, and the thesis when it can be done in only one way. Each box counts its credits against the 15 of full-time study. Below them, the courses of one study period at a time, with their pass rate and a survey column as in the list, sorted by either. The compulsory courses sit among the courses to choose between, sorted with them, with a lock in place of the button that adds a course, and cannot be removed. The year opens on the first study period with room and courses to choose from. A column's header flips the order. Each course shows its credits, and its seats when its round limits them. Adding a course puts it in its study periods, and a course that runs over two is marked so, shows how many of its credits fall in the listed period, and counts in each what it gives there. A course already in the year in another study period is greyed out, and adding it there moves it. A course picked in another year is greyed out with its year. The planner says when two courses share lecture times, when only one of two courses counts towards the degree, and how far the courses of every year go towards a compulsory elective rule, also a course the plan lists outside the rule in another year. Below those rules sit the degree requirements the student has to choose courses for, its lists of courses before its themes, each with how far the compulsory courses and the picks of every year go towards it. Each compulsory elective rule and each list of courses has its own shape, in the order they show: a dot, a triangle, a square, a ring, then a diamond. A course that counts towards one says so after its name: the rule's shape, or a label with its credits in the theme. The marks fade once the rule is met. Courses without results link to their syllabus on chalmers.se, and a course that only a course list names links to that list. A civil engineering programme can add a master's programme after year 3, from a dashed button beside the years. It opens a list of the master's programmes the programme syllabus accredits, each with its specialisations, its compulsory credits and courses, its number of electives, whether the student's courses meet what admission to it asks of the programme's students, and behind a button the courses and subjects its syllabus asks students to have studied before it. The chosen one adds years 4 and 5 with its own plan, and the degree requirements count its courses too. Under its name above every year sit what admission asks for and what is missing, with where the plans of years 1 to 3 give it, and its prerequisites behind a button. In years 1 to 3 each course requirement for admission is a rule of the year, with its shape on the courses that meet it in time. A button after year 5 changes it, and the picks of each master's programme stay remembered. The start year, the track, the picks and the master's programme live in the URL, so a plan can be shared. The browser also remembers them for each programme and year, and the planner link in the header returns to the programme and year last planned. A start year, a track, picks and a master's programme in a URL win over remembered ones.
- **Course**: a link to the syllabus on chalmers.se when it has one, which is for every course it lists since 2021/2022, then this year's study periods with their examiners and what the programmes that plan the course ask of it, then the four numbers, with this year's seats under the attempts per year, then the timeline at full width, then grades, other parts, the course survey, and other instances of the course. The survey lists every round and sums up the newest one, or another round or all of them when picked in the list.

## Definitions

- Pass rate: passed attempts divided by all attempts on the main exam, over the last three academic years with results.
- Size: attempts per year on the main exam, over the same three years.
- Ended: no plan has the course this year, and either chalmers.se no longer offers it or it has had no results for 18 months. chalmers.se offers a course in an academic year when it has a syllabus of the course for that year that does not discontinue it. Re-exams give results for years after a course's last round, so results alone do not keep a course running. A course that chalmers.se has not listed since 2021/2022 goes by its results.
- Main exam: the exam part with the most attempts. Courses without an exam use their largest part.
- Rating: the mean answer to "What is your overall impression of the course?" in Chalmers course surveys, 1 to 5, over every answer in the last three academic years with surveys. Courses change, so older rounds stay out, also of the course page. Courses with fewer than five answers have no rating. The survey reports only show how many gave each answer as a bar chart per question, so `pnpm evaluations` reads the bar lengths of every standard question and checks them against the reported mean.
- Trend: the rating per academic year over the last five years with surveys, on the fixed 1 to 5 scale, so rows compare. A year with fewer than five answers is left out. Sorting by trend compares the last year with the mean of up to three years before it.
- Spread: marks for the rating and the other survey questions shade each answer by its share, darkest from 60% of answers, blending between neighbouring answers, with a tick at the median. Average grade shows only its number, since the pass rate bar beside it shows how the grades spread. Each whole step of the scale gets an equal slot, so values at the ends sit inside the mark. Mean, median and standard deviation show on hover.
- Compulsory elective: an elective course in a choice of the programme plan that asks for at least some of its courses. The plans have no rule of their own for it.
- Degree requirement: what the programme syllabus asks of the whole degree on top of the yearly plans. The planner knows two kinds: credits in a theme, which is MTS (Human, technology and society) or Environment and sustainable development, and at least one course of a list. A course often gives fewer theme credits than its own credits. The planner leaves out a requirement that the programme's compulsory courses meet on their own, since it asks nothing of the student. A requirement that a master's programme's compulsory courses meet stays, shown as met, since the choice of master's programme meets it. A programme without a syllabus for the year takes its newest one from the five years before.
- Start year in the planner: the academic year a student started the programme in, from 2021/2022, the first year chalmers.se has plans for. The degree requirements and accredited master's programmes come from that year's programme syllabus. Each year of study follows the plan of the academic year the student takes it in, or the newest plan before it that has the year, so years not planned yet follow the current plan. The planner asks for it, since the newest is easy to keep by mistake, unless the link or the browser names one or the programme admitted students in one year only.
- Master's programme in the planner: a civil engineering programme's fourth and fifth year, whatever years its own plan has this year. The programme syllabus of the start year lists the accredited ones. The master's programme starts three years after the programme, and its years follow the plans of that year on. When Chalmers has not published them yet, the planner shows the current master's programmes with a note that the ones on offer can change. When Chalmers has announced the master's programmes of a later year, the planner shows those instead, from its accreditation matrix and each one's course list. A master's programme that leads to the degree only with some specialisations says which. Its courses are the ones on its course list that a plan gives this year, in the study periods they run in now. A name on the list matches a course whatever its spaces, hyphens and punctuation. The rest, such as new courses and the thesis, have no study periods or credits yet. They sit under Not scheduled, beside the study periods, where they can be picked like any other course, and a year with such courses shows all four study periods. A compulsory course that runs in several study periods is taken once, in the one the student picks. A plan's picks are kept by the master's programme and its own year, so the same picks show whether it is planned alone or after a programme.
- Admission in the planner: what Chalmers' eligibility guide for its own students, "Val av masterprogram för Chalmersstudenter – Behörighetsguide", asks of a programme's students for a master's programme: nothing beyond the compulsory courses of years 1 to 3, courses by their codes, or what the admission regulations ask in subjects and credits. The planner checks the codes against the compulsory courses and the picks of years 1 to 3. A course counts once it ends in study period 3 of year 3 at the latest, since admission checks the courses in April, unless the guide marks it as one that only needs a registration. Admission can also accept other courses like these, and the planner does not guess which. The guide of the year the master's programme starts in applies, and until it is out the newest one does, with a note. A specialisation that students apply to on its own has requirements of its own, which apply when the plan follows it. What the guide says in words that name no courses shows as its words, without a check.
- Block: Chalmers' timetable slot of a course round, A to D. Two courses in the same block in the same study period have lectures at the same times. A plus, as in "B+", adds hours to the block, so the planner counts it as the block. About half of the rounds have a block.
- Thesis in the planner: thesis courses that run in the same study periods for the same credits fill the year alike, so they show as one. A year with one such thesis has it in the year from the start. A year with several, such as a thesis of 30 or of 60 credits, lists them to choose between.
- Workload: the course survey question on workload, where 1 is too low, 3 balanced and 5 too high. It stays out of the rating. Sorting by it starts with the lightest.
- Survey column: the mean answer to one standard course survey question, the overall impression unless another is picked, over the same answers as the rating. A question with fewer than five answers gives no value, and those courses sort last.
- Examiner: who examines a course's rounds this academic year, from the course syllabi behind the programme plans. Courses that no plan has this year have none.
- Seats: how many students a course round takes this academic year, from the same course syllabi. About half of the courses in this year's plans limit them. The planner shows them on each course to choose, and the course page under the attempts per year, by study period. A round without a limit, and a year of study that follows an earlier academic year's plan, shows none.
- Study period filter: the study periods a course runs in this academic year, from the programme plans. Courses that no plan has this year match none.
